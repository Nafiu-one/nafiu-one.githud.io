CHAPTER 1



The sun rose lazily over Brightwood High School, casting pale gold light across the sprawling compound. The morning air buzzed with chatter, laughter, and the endless noise of over a thousand teenagers rushing toward their various classes. For most students, it was just another school day.But for Musa Danjuma, today already felt different.Musa wasn’t the flashy type—he wasn’t loud, dramatic, or effortlessly popular. He was just… Musa. Quiet. Brilliant. Preferably unnoticed. His happiest moments happened in the robotics workshop or tucked away in the library with a physics textbook thicker than a brick.As he walked toward the assembly ground, he noticed something unusual: groups of students whispering intensely, pointing toward the principal’s office.“What happened?” Musa asked one of the juniors passing by.

“You haven’t heard?” the boy whispered dramatically. “We have a transfer student.”

Musa shrugged. “People transfer all the time.”“No, no,” the boy insisted, eyes wide. “This one was expelled from his former school.” Expelled?That word snagged Musa’s attention.Before he could ask more, the bell rang sharply, and everyone rushed to assembly. The courtyard filled instantly—rows of blue uniforms, white shirts, ties askew, prefects shouting instructions that nobody listened to.The principal, Mr. Adeboye, stepped up to the podium. His strict posture softened slightly as he adjusted his glasses.“Good morning, students,” he began. “Today, we welcome a new student who will be joining SS2 Science, Room 12.”Musa’s class. “This is David Armstong

A tall boy stepped forward. His skin was fair, his hair dark and unkempt, and his face… unreadable. Like a shuttered window with something flickering behind it. His eyes held a quiet burn—anger? Pain? Musa couldn’t tell.David didn’t wave or smile.He simply nodded once, then stepped back.Yet the entire assembly buzzed.Everyone felt it:Something about this boy was different.When the class filed into Room 12 after assembly, the chatter was already wild.“I heard he punched a teacher!”“No, I heard he hacked the school computer!”“I heard he burned down a lab!”

Musa sighed. Teenagers loved exaggerated drama.When David walked in, the room fell eerily silent. Thirty pairs of curious eyes followed him

Mrs. Makinde, the Math teacher, pointed to the empty seat beside Musa. “Armstrong, sit there.”David nodded and dropped into the seat without a glance "Good morning,” Musa said politely.David didn’t answer. He was staring at a notebook already open on his desk. Musa glanced at iIt wasn’t math. It wasn’t notes It was symbols, diagrams, shapes, numbers—drawn with sharp, restless strokes. One symbol repeated itself again and again: three circles inside a triangle. Before Musa could say anything, David snapped the notebook shut.“It’s nothing,” he muttered.But Musa knew it wasn’t nothing.

At break time, chaos exploded near the basketball court. Teachers ran. Students screamed. Prefects blew whistles.The school’s generator room had been torn apart—wires ripped, bolts unscrewed, fuel spilled everywhere.And on the wall, someone had drawn the same symbol Musa saw in David’s notebook:Three circles.Inside a triangle.Even Musa froze.People pointed fingers quickly.“It must be the new boy!”“He looks like trouble!”“Check his bag!”David stood alone, staring at the symbol with a strange mix of shock and fury—not fear, not guilt.That night, the entire school buzzed with rumours.Musa lay awake long after lights-out, staring at the ceiling.He wasn’t convinced David did it.But if he didn’t… who did?The next day, Musa found David sitting alone behind the library, knees drawn up, staring into nothing.Musa approached carefully.“Did you do it?” he asked softly.David snorted. “If I did, do you think I’d leave my signature on the wall?”“So what is that symbol?”David hesitated for several seconds. Long enough for Musa to start regretting asking.“It’s an engineering mark,” David finally said. His voice softened. “My dad used to teach me special symbols for circuits… before he died.”Musa’s chest tightened.“Sorry.”David shrugged. “It’s fine. People think I’m bad. I’m used to it.”“You didn’t answer my question,” Musa said gently. “Why do you draw it everywhere?”David stared at the ground.“Because it’s the last thing my father designed… and the only thing I have left from him.”Musa sat down beside him.In that moment, something changed.Two quiet worlds touched.Two lonely boys found a crack in their walls.And a friendship began.One week later:The school’s water tank burst mysteriously.The computer lab system crashed overnight.The Home Economics kitchen gas pipe was tampered with.Each time, the strange symbol appeared somewhere nearby.Everyone grew suspicious.Everyone accused David.Even teachers eyed him warily.But Musa knew.This sabotage required engineering skills.Real, technical expertise.Someone else was behind it.And they were framing David.One evening, Musa stayed late to finish a robotics project. As he packed up, he noticed something unusual:Mr. Johnson, the school’s maintenance officer, was behind Block F, drawing something on a plank of wood.The same symbol.Exactly the same.Musa’s heart pounded.He ran to find David—found him alone in Room 12.Showed him what he saw.David’s face went pale.“They’re framing me,” he whispered.“We need proof,” Musa said.“And we’ll get it tonight.”At 8:10 pm, after night prep ended, Musa and David snuck out of the hostel. They moved like shadows across the field, crouching behind walls when prefects passed.They reached Block F and hid behind the workshop.Inside, they heard voices.“…once the school fails again, we’ll fix it and double the charges,” Mr. Johnson said.Another man replied, “Yes. Just blame the new student. Everyone already suspects him.”David’s fists clenched so tightly his knuckles turned white.Musa whispered, “We need to record this.”He lifted his phone to capture the audio.But suddenly—CLANG!A metal rod fell off a shelf.The voices inside stopped.“Who’s there?”Footsteps.Fast. Heavy.“RUN!” Musa shouted.The boys bolted—feet slamming the floor, lungs burning, hearts pounding. They dashed around a corner, ducked under windows, sprinted across the field.But the men chased them.“Catch them!”Musa grabbed David’s wrist and pulled.They were almost at the hostel when a flashlight blinded them.“Stop where you are!”It was the chief security guard.Finally—They were safe.After a long night of questioning, Musa played the recording.The entire plot unraveled.Mr. Johnson and a contractor had been sabotaging the school for months to secure inflated repair contracts. They used the strange symbol to mislead the school and divert suspicion toward David.The next morning, the principal made an announcement.“David Armstrong is innocent.”Students apologized.Teachers apologized.Even the prefects apologized.But the most important thing was the smile David gave Musa—relieved, grateful, soft.Something new lived in his eyes now:trust.
